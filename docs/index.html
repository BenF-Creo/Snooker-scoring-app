Snooker Rules

Objective

Score more points than your opponent in a frame by potting balls in the correct order. A match is the best of an agreed number of frames.

Balls & values

  • Red — 1
  • Yellow — 2
  • Green — 3
  • Brown — 4
  • Blue — 5
  • Pink — 6
  • Black — 7

The standard game has 15 reds and the 6 colours. A shorter variant is played with 10 reds.

The break-off & safety

Each frame begins with a break-off — the first player plays from inside the “D” into the pack of reds. Players alternate the break-off in each frame of a match.

The opening exchanges are often safety play: shots played to leave the opponent nothing to pot rather than to score, until a player is able to commit to building a break.

Playing a frame

  1. While reds remain, you must pot a red first (1 point), then a colour of your choice. Reds stay down once potted; colours are returned to their spots.
  2. Keep alternating red, colour, red, colour… Your turn (your break) continues as long as you keep potting legally.
  3. Once all reds are gone, pot the final colour, then clear the six colours in ascending order: yellow, green, brown, blue, pink, black. These are not respotted.

Breaks

A break is the number of points scored in a single visit to the table. A break of 100 or more is a century; the highest possible break is 147.

The “ball on”

The ball on is whatever you’re allowed to hit first and pot: a red when reds are on, then a colour, and so on. Hitting or potting anything that isn’t the ball on is a foul.

Fouls

A foul ends your turn and gives your opponent penalty points — the greater of 4 and the value of the ball involved (so 5 for the blue, 6 for the pink, 7 for the black). Common fouls:

  • Failing to hit the ball on first (or hitting nothing at all).
  • Potting the wrong ball, or a ball that isn’t on.
  • Potting the cue ball (in-off).
  • Forcing a ball off the table.
  • Touching a ball with anything other than the tip of the cue, or touching/moving any ball by hand, cue or clothing.
  • Playing a push shot, a jump shot, or playing out of turn / before the balls have stopped.
  • Playing with both feet off the floor, or using a ball off the table.

After a foul the incoming player may play the balls where they lie, or ask the offender to play again.

Free ball & misses

Free ball: if a foul leaves you snookered (you can’t hit both edges of the ball on), you may nominate any ball as the ball on for one shot, scoring its value as if it were the ball on.

Miss: if a player doesn’t make a genuine attempt to hit the ball on, a miss may be called — the penalty is awarded and the opponent can ask for the shot to be replayed.

Winning

The player with more points once the black is potted wins the frame (a player who is well behind may concede). The match is won by the first player to reach a majority of the frames. If scores are level after the black, it is re-spotted for a deciding shot.

Billiards Rules

Objective

English Billiards is played with three balls: two cue balls (a plain white and a spot white — one per player) and a red object ball. You keep your own cue ball all game. First to the agreed target score wins.

Ways to score

  • Cannon — your cue ball strikes both other balls in one stroke: 2 points.
  • Pot red (winning hazard): 3 points.
  • In-off red (losing hazard — your cue ball into a pocket after hitting the red): 3 points.
  • Pot opponent’s white: 2 points.
  • In-off white: 2 points.

Several scores can be made in one stroke and all count — for example a cannon that also sends the cue ball in-off, or a cannon followed by a pot. A cannon combined with an in-off off the red is a classic “five” (2 + 3). To record a stroke like this, just tap each score in turn — e.g. Cannon then In-off red — and the break adds up.

Break-off & turns

One player breaks off to start, the red being spotted and the opponent’s cue ball in hand. A player keeps the table, adding to their break, until they fail to score; the turn then passes to the opponent.

Fouls

A foul scores nothing for the striker, ends the turn and gives 2 points to the opponent (tap Foul +2). A stroke is a foul if you:

  • Fail to hit any object ball with your cue ball.
  • Strike when the balls aren’t at rest, or play with the wrong cue ball.
  • Force any ball off the table.
  • Touch or move a ball other than by a fair stroke, or make a push or jump shot.
  • Play with both feet off the floor.

The coup

A coup is when your cue ball goes into a pocket without first hitting another ball. It is not a scoring in-off — it is a foul (2 points to the opponent), and the cue ball is then in hand for the next player.

Running a coup is the in-hand version: playing from the “D” straight into a pocket when there’s no ball to aim at out of baulk. Once allowed only as a “miss”, it has been a foul in the amateur game since 2001. In the app, record any coup with the Foul +2 button.

Repetition limits

To stop endless identical breaks, the rules cap repeated scoring:

  • Consecutive cannons: no more than 75 in a row.
  • Consecutive hazards (pots and in-offs): no more than 15 without a cannon.
  • Spot-stroke (potting the red repeatedly): limited to two in succession from its own spot before it is spotted on the centre spot.

Winning

Players agree a target score before the game (commonly 100–300). The first player to reach the target wins. Games may also be played to an agreed time, the higher score winning.
